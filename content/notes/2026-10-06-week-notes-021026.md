### Cambridge Conservation Initiative Town Hall

To kick off the new year we had our CCI town hall meeting, in which the CCI executive team lay out where we are going in the next year. Over the last year there has been a consolidation of the CCI brand, in a literal sense as well as in a strategy sense. We now have a nice pink and blue logo, which actually [looks very nice](https://www.cambridgeconservation.org/).  

As we go into the next year, the focus of CCI appears to be on four things. 

1. The first is convening relevant stakeholders to put nature at the heart of decision making, especially in the context of a post [30x30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_by_30) agenda, and AI-Nature pathways - something that is being headed up by Chris Sandbrook. 
2. The second is 'Transformative Restoration', with a specfic focus on getting support for a West Africa programme, as this is a region that many members of the CCI work in. The aim is to build on the apparent success of the [Endangered Landscapes and Seascape Programme](https://www.endangeredlandscapes.org/), which is also hosted in CCI. 
3. The third element is further investing in the [Arts, Science on Conservation Programme](https://www.cambridgeconservation.org/our-work/arts-programme/), making it a bigger part of the CCI. We now have a new planning and evaluation framework for our resident artists - which seems like an interesting thing to impose on artists. 
4. The fourth is improving the CCI collaborative fund. This fund is the main mechanism through which the CCI can catalyse impactful work across the organisations in CCI - all the organisations would love to work together more - but at the end of the day the there needs to be a cost code on a timesheet that accounts for everyone's time. So far the collaborative fund has funded 76 projects to the tune of £4.5 million.

### A new project with Tom Worthington's group

I have been in conversations with [Emma Deeks](https://www.globalcoastalwetlands.com/the-team), a postdoc in [Tom's Global Coastal Wetlands Lab](https://www.globalcoastalwetlands.com/the-team). Emma has a student starting who will be undertaking a project, using our LLM 'knowhow', to try and glean from the literature an insight into the species that use and rely on saltmarsh habitats. We are hoping to be able to use our unique literature access and classification work to identify papers which discuss this topic and create a georeferenced list of salt marshes and the species that are using them.
