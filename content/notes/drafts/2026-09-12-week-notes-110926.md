### Meeting with Pew, Esmee Fairburn and the Wildlife Trusts

On Monday we had a meeting with Pew, Esmee Faifburn and the Wildlife Trusts to discuss the Conservation Evidence Copilot. We did a demonstration of its functionality and discussed how it could possibly be integrated into conservation practice. The Wildlife Trust were especially interested, as the copilots ability to audit its decision making, and its closed focus on the Conservation Evidence Database could make it a useful tool for practitioners. As the Wildlife Trust are currently warning its members off using zero-shot proprietary LLMs, due to the lack of transparency in their decision making processes. We also discussed the possibility of introducing cost estimate information into the tool - however, we will need to think how we can best integrate this in a reliable manner. 



### Ai4Nature Monthly Meeting

We had our monthly co-founders meeting of the Ai4Nature Alliance, discussing our aims for the end of the year. We are planning to gather more case studies of AI use across the environmental industry and research, as well as organising our end of year 'festive mixer' in December. 



### DEFRA's Data and Information Festival

Sadiq and I did one our 45 minute prepared talks for DEFRA's interestingly named "Data and Information Festival". This was online, with over 100 speakers taking part across 56 sessions over the course of the week. Our session was on the inevitable rise of AI in government decision making, and what decisions and pitfalls you need to be aware of when trying to responsibly integrate AI into your workflows, with particular focus on evidence synthesis and decision making. We had somewhere between 200 and 300 attendees, and spoke for ~40 minutes. We had some nice feedback and good questions. It seems that there is some frustration among the researchers at DEFRA that they would like to be doing more, and at a faster pace, but feel a bit hamstrung by internal red-tape that makes researching and deploying these technologies more difficult. It is times like these that I feel fortunate to be working in academia, where we have a lot of flexibility to be able to explore these models capabilities.
