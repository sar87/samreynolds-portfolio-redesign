title: AI in San Francisco - a bubble in a bubble or window to the future?

San Francisco is a strange city. Robot taxis, unapologetic capitalism, AI hype and harrowing homelessness all nestled in a small peninsula. The entrepreneur spirit is rife in a way that you don't really experience in the UK, there is certainly no tall poppy syndrome among the young people cramming out the Corgi Cafe. There are a few observations and conversations I thought might be worth capturing while they are still relatively fresh in my mind:

## A chat with Anthropic

I was fortunate enough to corner an employee at Anthropic and pick their brains about where they see AI going, and whether we in conservation are thinking big enough. This employee will remain nameless, but is, by their own admission, "fully AGI pilled". 

My first question was, if AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) is coming, where do we spend our time? The paraphrased answer was along the lines of: 

> *Organising data and collecting data. If you think that the application you are thinking of could be 'one-shotted' in a years time, don't waste time building it now and focus on gathering and organising the underlying data*. 

This makes sense to me following my experience building the Conservation Evidence Copilot, the reason that the Copilot can deliver efficiently for users is because we have spent 20 years laboriously collating the data in a way that makes sense. The actual building of the Copilot tool, in comparison to that evidence gathering effort, is relatively trivial. 

When I asked about whether we should be using AI in conservation, the answer was certainly one of an American perspective: 

> *Do you want to be fighting with one hand behind your back? Other organisations who will be working against the goals of conservation organisations will and are using this technology - to fight against these organisations would be an uphill battle without embracing these tools in some capacity*. 

We moved away from conservation slightly to chat about where they see AI going. 

> *Changes in society are coming, and the white collar jobs will be the first to go. There will be push back from these groups, and it is not something we have seen before in society. How these groups decide to organise, how they decide to push back will be highly relevant to the changes we see in the near future.* 

This isn't necessarily a new perspective, but it certainly the first time I have heard it stated in person with such certainty. It always seems that 'this time is different', but in this case it does feel that way. This will not be the marginalisation of already marginalised groups which have historically been swept under the giant rug of 'progress', these are powerful people in high skilled jobs that value knowledge: such as lawyers, civil servants, accountants, financial analysts, the list goes on. These groups of people are going to experience what software engineers have over the last year, and now mathematicians are rallying against. In some ways, whether AI can actually perform these jobs better than people probably matters less, in the short term, than the perception that they can. 

We all know that in all jobs there are nuances that are perhaps not fully recognised or valued by managers, the problem is, AI gives people managing teams the ability create tools that appear to do the job of their employees. It can give the illusion of competence to those without the competencies. Anyone who has used AI coding agents will know the feeling of creating a piece of software that makes you feel incredibly empowered. However, if a real software engineer was to look at the code, they would probably cry at how poorly it is strung together. If, like I have, you have worked with AI in your own area of research, you realise that in a lot of ways it is pretty stupid, but as soon as you step away from your own area of expertise it seems all powerful. This is the Gell-Mann Amnesia Effect - you read a newspaper article on a topic you know intimately and spot dozens of errors, oversimplifications, or complete misunderstandings. Yet, you turn the page and read an article on a completely different topic and swallow it whole, forgetting how badly the paper botched the subject you actually understood. If you are in a decision making position that is sufficiently distant to the details of specific details that matter, it is perhaps feasible that you might make the decision replace teams of people with AI under the illusion the output you are getting is 'the same'. 

We then moved on to discussing the state of AI use within Anthropic, and their concerns around security and alignment.
