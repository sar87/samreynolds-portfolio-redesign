### An interesting time to be in the 'AI space'

With lots of chatter about model alignment (what you ask a model to do vs. what a model will do to achieve the results), frontier capabilities, and the dangers of autonomous agents - there are a lot of topics to sink your teeth into. It's hard to know whether to still feel excited or existential about AI. There is a broad understanding that this technology is going to fundamentally change how we work, research and live our lives, but how exactly that will manifest is less clear (to me at least). It is hard to have conversations about AI from a standing start, as you can often end up talking cross purposes. I am trying to use some of my time off to clarify my thinking on AI in Conservation and AI more broadly. 

There are big differences regarding the implications of a geo-observational model like [TESSERA](https://geotessera.org/), or a small classifier used to distinguish bird calls, and frontier LLMs that are being used to power agentic bot swarms chasing after a goal at any cost. However, they are often all captured under the same broad umbrella in conversation by the term 'AI'. Or am I wrong to think there are significant differences? Am I wrong to think that the impact of these technologies can be separated? Should they be considered in the same breath? These are slightly difficult things to untangle. 

There are some criticisms that can be levelled at all AI models to various degrees e.g., transparency, resources used in training and inference, representativeness of training data. Some are specific to certain types of models e.g., a bird classifier is not going to hack the Australian Government, whereas misaligned frontier LLMs might. However, the values you hold as an individual, or as an organisation or government, might affect how you decide to view and implement AI more broadly. The existential threat of agentic bot swarms, the concentration of corporations controlling this frontier technology, and the behaviour of chip makers that we are reliant on for training and deploying all forms of AI application, large or small, might mean that you do not want to support any part of it. This is a decision that you need to come to yourself, but I feel is a decision that can be swayed one way or the other depending on how you think/feel about the specific AI application, the behaviours and motivations of model developers, the infrastructure providers, the politics and the broader societal conversation. 

I think we need some kind of framework to be able to sense check our own understanding and feelings about AI, in conservation and beyond. A bit like [Andrew Balmford's](https://www.zoo.cam.ac.uk/people/andrew-balmford-frs) feelings (as I understand them) that doing effective conservation is all very well and good, but the main driver or biodiversity loss is land use change. If you can get a handle on affecting land use decisions, you can do more for conservation than the most effective conservation interventions might do. The same is likely true for AI, as highlighted by [Chris Sandbrook](https://www.geog.cam.ac.uk/people/sandbrook/), we could make the most powerful AI tools to aid conservation efforts that we have ever seen, but if frontier AI models fundamentally change how societies, economies and politics function, these changes are likely to outweigh any positive movement we can make in conservation.

This is all from the top of my head, through my fingers, and into your eyes - more considered thoughts to follow (hopefully).

### Future of the Conservation Evidence Website

Will Morgan and I have been meeting with some database specialists to figure out how we can move the Conservation Evidence (CE) [website](www.conservationevidence.com) into the 21st Century. The CE website has been built slowly and iteratively over many years. The initial structure that was developed is now creaking, and as well as a larger number of autonomous bot accesses to the website, the structure of the data is leading to frequent hitches in website performance. 

Luckily, we have found two people that we are looking to bring on board to help us transition the data structures underlying the CE website - this should not only make the website more stable - but also make the data, and how we serve that data, more flexible. This will improve our ability to be able to integrate CE data into third party tools, such as [IBAT](https://www.ibat-alliance.org/).
