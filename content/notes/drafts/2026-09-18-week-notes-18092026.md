### CCI Catalyst Fund Kick-Off Session

On Tuesday we had the kick off 'introduction' session for the CCI (Cambridge Conservation Initiative) Catalyst Fund. I was fortunate enough to secure £20,000 funding from the latest round of the fund, along with my co-leads on the grant, Chris Sandbrook (Department of Geography, University of Cambridge, and head of the Conservation Research Initiative) and Stu Butchart (Chief Scientist at Birdlife International). The aim of our grant is to work with the other CCI partners, other NGOs, computer scientists and sustainability experts to collectively assess near-term opportunities for using AI in conservation practice and operations, critically evaluate the environmental and social costs of AI adoption, and begin development of aligned CCI principles for responsible AI use. Outputs include a peer-reviewed publication, white paper and organisational action plans.

This will be a really interesting project to run, but I imagine it will also be quite a lot of work to organise and run effectively. We are looking to invite around 40 people to this two-day workshop. We are aiming to hold this before the end of April 2027 - but I am yet to actually propose or confirm this with the others involved in this project.

My colleagues in Conservation Evidence, Will Morgan and Nigel Taylor, also secured funding in this round of CCI funding. Will is looking to work with IBAT, a tool that is primarily used by businesses to assess the biodiversity impact of their business footprint, to integrate the Conservation Evidence database into their product. The tool is currently a fantastic way of businesses understanding their risks, but has little to offer in the way of solutions. This is where we (as Conservation Evidence) see ourselves fitting in. Nigel's project looks to extend a project a previous CCI project we had funded on a database for invasive species management actions. Working with IUCN and Birdlife international, as well as in-kind assistance from other CCI organisations Nigel is looking to begin populating this database and put together a minimal viable product.

### Meeting with Karl Burkart (CEO of Nature Data Lab)

On Wednesday Karl and I finally managed to find a time to talk AI and Conservation. Karl originally reached out to me a couple of months ago in respect to an IUCN White Paper on AI in Conservation that he is putting together. Karl was interested to talk to me off the back of my AI Horizon Scan paper, published early 2025. The catalyst for this meeting was that we will both be attending the Unusual Dialogue at Stanford University on Harnessing AI for Nature, at the beginning of October. 

We had a great chat ranging from the work we are doing here at Cambridge on mobilising information from academic publications, to TESSERA, to my vision of where we see our current research going (See Blog), to his time as Director of Innovation, Media & Technology for the Leonardo DiCaprio Foundation (whom he refers to as 'Leo'), and expressing our simultaneous excitement and concern for the potential of AI in the conservation space. 

I will be helping to draft this white paper for IUCN on AI in conservation, which is being supported by a survey that has been completed by ~500 conservationists on their thoughts and fears of AI in the conservation space. I am very much looking forward to being part of this process.
