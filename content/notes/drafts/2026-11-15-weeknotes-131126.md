### CSaP Workshop

On Monday we held a workshop in collaboration with the [Cambridge Centre for Science and Policy](https://www.csap.cam.ac.uk/) (CSaP). This has been in the works for a few months now, so it is great to finally be able to debut the coplot and get some valuable feedback from potential users.

![](/images/notes/Image-1.jpeg)

The day started with a 45 minute lunch and networking session, and led into a presentation by myself on the theory behind how the [Conservation Evidence Copilot](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j9b3_stSB8M) (CEC) works. The participants (who represent government, industry, and practice) were then able to try the app themselves. With some impeccable timing, the app crashed almost immediately - despite us taking several steps the week before to stress test the VM that hosts the app the weak before. Fortunately, with some desperate communications to the incredible Mark Elvers back in Computer Science, we were able to get the app up and running again for the rest of the day. 

During our feedback session, we received some really great feedback on potential features, use cases, limitations and insights into what it would take for different user groups to be able to use the CEC for their work. I will share a broader report on the outcomes of the workshop next week when I receive the full CSaP report. 

The rest of the day consisted of a round table discussion (as pictured above) on the broader use of AI for decision making in their respective organisations. Tapping into their hopes, concerns and what limitations they face for embracing these technological advances. 

The day culminated in a dinner, expertly chaired by CSaP's Philip Guildford, who kindly came out of retirement to host both the roundtable and the dinner. Alongside our food and wine we considered what steps we would need to take to make sure we could offer the CEC to a wide range of users through both paid for and free models. Our intention is to make this tool free to the majority, while perhaps offering some more business facing capabilities at some cost to users that can afford to pay.  

### Conservation Evidence  No-Way Day

We held the first CE No-Way Day, in replacement for our usual annual away day. This year we stayed in Cambridge and I booked us a room in Pembroke college supplied throughout the day with tea, coffee, biscuits and lunch. 

We discussed many aspects of upcoming work in CE. Chiefly our potential integration with [IBAT](https://www.ibat-alliance.org/). Which is an alliance of CCI organisations who make their data available to businesses in order for them to carry out risk assessments. We believe there is a lot of value in the integration of Conservation Evidence, as it along with the current ability to identify risks and threats, CE would be able to highlight potential 'solutions'. Or rather, actions that a developer could take to help to mitigate their impact on the environment when it comes to biodiversity. 

We also discussed many other things that are slightly drier in nature, but are incredibly important for how the group can function into the long term. Which includes setting up routes through which we can more easily collaborate with interested organisations and accept smaller pots of funding for discrete projects. Which is currently very difficult to do in a university setting. 

### Senior Research Fellowship Application

On Tuesday this week I tightened up and submitted my SRF application to the Department of Zoology. This is a slightly obscure procedure though which we have to win departmental approval to be able to apply for senior research fellowships. Every year, we can apply with a relatively short application form, the second round is presenting a talk to the department about your planned research. If you make it past these hurdles, the department and School of Biology will help you to prepare applications for senior research fellowships that will allow you to be hosted in the department. Each year the department takes on ~two people. I managed to squeak mine in by the deadline, we will see by the beginning of December if I was successful in being invited to give a seminar.
